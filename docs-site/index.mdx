Attribution graphs from [circuit-tracer](https://github.com/decoderesearch/circuit-tracer) show which transcoder
features carry influence toward a model's output. Three questions come next, and circuitkit answers each one:

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Graph diff" icon="code-compare" href="/guides/diff">
    Which features and edges did fine-tuning or RL add or remove? Comes with a random null, a stability check and a
    validity check.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Circuit validator" icon="flask" href="/guides/validate">
    Is a group of features a **switch** that causes the behaviour, or a **readout** that only reflects it?
  </Card>
  <Card title="Feature labels" icon="tags" href="/guides/labels">
    A short label per feature from Claude Haiku 5.5, scored on held-out examples, for about half a cent a feature.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Why it exists

In two of steerlab's circuits studies, the graph pointed confidently at ten features. One was
[a password lock in Qwen3-8B](https://albertlieyingadrian.github.io/steerlab/experiments/lock-circuit/report/), the
other [an RL reward hack in Qwen3-1.7B](https://albertlieyingadrian.github.io/steerlab/experiments/rl-hack-circuit/report/).
Switching those ten off changed nothing: they read the behaviour but did not drive it. Telling the two apart took
controls, nulls and checks that circuit-tracer leaves to the user. circuitkit packages them.

<Note>
  Everything except the real-model backend runs without a GPU. The toy model, the bundled graphs of the RL-hack study
  and the cached label replies let you try every tool on a laptop in a few minutes.
</Note>
